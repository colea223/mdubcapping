About the Model

What actually goes into every rating, spread, and total on this site -- no black boxes.

Overview

This site runs four separate statistical models side by side, plus one published third-party rating used for reference. Only ONE of them is ever the live, actionable pick -- the rest are tracked purely for comparison, so their track record stays honest instead of getting blended into a single number after the fact.

Dub Gamma
The live model. Every spread, win probability, and "Model Edge" shown on Matchups/Predictions/the Ratings page is Dub Gamma's.
Ridge Model
A regularized linear-regression candidate, tracked alongside Gamma for comparison. Informational only.
Dub Beta Model
A gradient-boosted-trees candidate on the same inputs as Ridge. Informational only.
Totals Model
A separate regression that predicts the combined Over/Under total -- spread and total are different prediction problems, so this runs independently of the three margin models above.
SP+ (Off / Def / ST)
CFBD's own published efficiency ratings, shown on the Ratings page as a reference split -- not something this project models itself.
Dub Gamma Model -- the live model

Gamma is deliberately simple: every FBS team gets one rating, and that rating updates itself after every completed game using nothing but that game's own result. There's no black-box fitting step and no hidden weights -- the entire update is one formula, applied the same way to every team, every week.

The starting point. Every team enters the season with a one-time preseason strength estimate (a published industry power-rating number) -- used only as the very first Week 1 anchor. From that point forward, the season's own results are what actually move each team's number; the preseason estimate is never touched or re-referenced again once real games start.

The update, after every completed game. Each of the two teams in a game gets its rating updated independently using:

  • Net score -- that team's own scoring margin in the game (positive for a win, negative for a loss).
  • The opponent's rating heading into that specific game (not their current, most up-to-date rating -- the number they actually carried in at kickoff).
  • An injury differential -- that week's reported-out players' game-status weight (Out / Doubtful / Questionable / Probable) multiplied by each player's own season-long efficiency, summed per team, home minus away. A best-effort adjustment: a team with no injury report that week simply gets a neutral 0.

Those three combine into one number -- call it that game's "performance level": net score, plus the opponent's rating, plus the injury differential. The team's new rating is then 90% its old rating, blended with 10% of that game's performance level. In plain terms: one game never swings a team's rating very far, but a whole season of results gradually pulls a team's number toward wherever its actual play has been.

Turning ratings into a spread. The predicted spread for any matchup is simply the away team's rating minus the home team's rating, adjusted by a fixed home-field edge (worth a few points, favoring the home team). The favorite is whichever side has the lower number once that adjustment is applied.

Turning ratings into a win probability. The predicted margin is run through a normal probability curve to get a win percentage for each side. The "spread" of that curve (how much real games actually tend to miss the model's own predicted margin by) is re-measured from this season's own completed, graded games every time the model runs -- not a fixed assumption, so it gets more accurate to itself as the season goes on.

How updating actually works. Nothing about a team's rating is saved between pipeline runs. Every single time the pipeline runs, Gamma replays the entire season from scratch -- starting from every team's Week 1 anchor and walking forward through every completed FBS game, in the order it was actually played, applying the update above one game at a time -- until it reaches today. That's what "the live rating" means: the full, deterministic result of every game that's happened so far this season, recomputed fresh every time, never a stored running number that could drift or get out of sync.

What data it uses: final scores of every completed FBS game (with home/away/neutral site noted), and weekly injury reports. Nothing from Vegas lines, recruiting rankings, or advanced per-play stats feeds into Gamma at all -- that's deliberate, so it stays an independent, transparent second opinion rather than something indirectly re-deriving the market's own number.

Ridge Model -- informational

A regularized linear regression (specifically RidgeCV, meaning its one tuning knob is picked automatically via cross-validation, never by hand) that predicts the home team's margin of victory from a couple dozen pre-game features, including:

  • An internal baseline power-rating gap between the two teams (an Elo-style rating, recomputed the same way every week from completed games -- this is a separate, simpler system from Dub Gamma above, used here only as one input feature among many).
  • Rest days, the road team's travel distance, and elevation change (a real factor for teams like Air Force and Wyoming).
  • Whether the game is at a neutral site, and whether it's a conference game.
  • Gaps in SP+ team strength, play-by-play efficiency (PPA), and recruiting talent between the two teams.
  • A Mountain West involvement flag -- added after a real backtest showed the model was giving too much home-field credit specifically whenever an MW team was on the field, in either direction. This flag corrects that bias.
  • Prior-season drive-based stats and situational splits (down-and-distance, red zone, explosive plays), plus a measure of each team's returning production from last season.

Ridge used to be the live pick before Gamma took over that role. It's still fit and tracked every week -- its line shows up informationally on the Results/Tracking pages -- purely so its real track record stays visible for comparison, never blended into Gamma's own number.

Dub Beta Model -- informational

Beta uses the exact same inputs as Ridge (see the list above), but runs them through gradient-boosted decision trees instead of a straight-line regression -- a model architecture that can pick up on non-linear interactions between features (e.g. "travel distance only matters when rest days are also short") that a linear model can't represent. Its own tuning knobs (tree depth, learning rate, number of trees, and more) are picked automatically via a randomized cross-validated search, the same "never hand-tuned" discipline Ridge's alpha gets.

Beta is an architecture experiment, not a replacement -- it exists to answer "does a more flexible model actually predict better than a simple one, on the same information?" It's tracked side by side with Ridge and Gamma, never the live pick.

Totals Model -- Over/Under

A separate Ridge regression (same cross-validated approach as above) that predicts the combined total points in a game, not who wins by how much -- spreads and totals are different questions, so this doesn't share Ridge's margin model at all. It's built from both teams':

  • Prior-season SP+ offensive and defensive ratings.
  • Prior-season play-by-play efficiency (PPA), offense and defense.
  • This season's own in-progress play-by-play efficiency, using only games through the week before the one being predicted -- so a totals prediction never has access to information from the future.

Unlike the margin models (which look at the gap between two teams), the totals model adds the two teams' own numbers together -- two elite offenses playing each other should project a high total even though the gap between them is small, and summing rather than differencing is what lets the model see that.

Ratings Page: Off / Def / Special Teams, SOS, SOR

The Net Rating column on the Ratings page is always Dub Gamma's -- the same live number used everywhere else on the site. Three other columns come from elsewhere:

  • Off / Def / ST are CFBD's own published SP+ efficiency ratings -- a well-established third-party measure, shown here as a reference split since Gamma itself only ever produces one overall number, not separate offense/defense/special-teams components.
  • SOS (Strength of Schedule) and SOR (Strength of Record) come from this project's own internal baseline rating system (the same Elo-style model mentioned under Ridge above) -- shown only as a rank, never a raw number, since they answer a resume/schedule-strength question rather than "how good is this team," which is what Net Rating is for.
  • Projection is Dub Gamma's own current win probability, summed across each team's remaining games this season, added to their current win total -- a projected final win count, not a playoff or seeding projection.
Data Sources
  • College Football Data (CFBD) -- final scores, schedules, play-by-play, drive-level stats, SP+ ratings, recruiting talent, and returning-production data for every FBS team.
  • Sportsbook lines -- opening and current spread, total, and moneyline, pulled from tracked sportsbooks for every game with a posted market.
  • Weekly injury reports -- best-effort, since there's no single reliable automated feed for this; a week with no report for a team is treated as no injury impact rather than an error.
  • One-time preseason strength estimate -- used only as Dub Gamma's Week 1 starting point for every team; never referenced again once the season's own results take over.
Every model here is recomputed from scratch on every pipeline run -- nothing is hand-adjusted after the fact.
